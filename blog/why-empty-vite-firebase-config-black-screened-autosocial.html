Why did a “finished” AutoSocial deploy hand buyers a black screen?

Production looked deployed and painted black. After the fix, a product that cannot boot is not allowed to pretend it is live.

Shane Foster · Founder, Haven Command LLC

What happens when production is a black screen?

Something that runs on one laptop. “It works” means their machine. A repo is handed over, or it is “deployed,” and the person who paid inherits a black screen. Nobody owns the week after.

The sentence is already known: “They built a PoC we can’t run in our stack.” The model never got a chance. The product never became itself.

What broke on AutoSocial?

Locally, AutoSocial loaded. In production it was a black screen. No useful error for the person who paid. The app “was deployed.” It was unusable.

Haven Command hit this on its own product — the same class of failure a client would have inherited on day one.

What did we change?

A product that comes up in the real environment, or a deploy that is not allowed to call itself finished. No quiet black screen dressed up as success.

That line is now on how we work: a product that cannot boot is not allowed to pretend it is live. AutoSocial is the evidence, not a deploy checklist.

A finished-looking AI app that paints black is not finished. The buyer just cannot see why.

Why does this show up in custom software?

Most custom applications die at the host. The studio already took a black screen on a live product and replaced it with something an operator can open. If you need a workflow that runs in the real environment, not a laptop, that is the job — see the product note.
