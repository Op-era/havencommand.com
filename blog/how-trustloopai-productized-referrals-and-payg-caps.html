Fail

They skip the meter. The demo is the loop. Referrals are a spreadsheet. Pay-as-you-go has no ceiling. A retry storm or a generous friend becomes an ops incident with a Stripe notification. Nobody owns billing.

That is the last mile buyers name out loud: “Nobody owns auth, billing, deploy, publish, admin.” You hired for AI. You got prompts and an unbounded invoice.

What broke in production?

Broke

Once TrustLoopAI could talk to real customers, usage became a product question. Referrals bring people in. PAYG feels fair. Neither is fair if a single loop can run the meter without a ceiling.

This pairs with model failover: a fallback that keeps the loop alive also keeps the meter running. Failover without a cap is how you donate margin to an outage. Haven Command would not invoice the surprise later.

What did Haven Command change?

Change

Caps and referral rules live in the product. PAYG has a ceiling. Referrals have terms the software can enforce. Billing is not a side quest after a long week.

Referral and PAYG-cap billing now sit in the systems library so the next usage product plugs into a proven ceiling instead of a spreadsheet.

If the only cap is your credit card alert, you did not finish the product.
