Why must a non-expert explanation still be honest?

A punchy explanation almost invented a blunder. After the fix, the human layer is still about the real game — or it does not run.

Shane Foster · Founder, Haven Command LLC

What happens when the explanation is punchy and false?

Specialist output dumped on the user is called transparency. A friendly voice that invents a story so it lands is called personality. Non-experts do the same thing either way: they ignore it, or they paste it into Slack and hope someone else checks.

The sentence a customer, an operator, or a club player will act on has to be true. If it is false, you shipped liability with a tone.

What almost shipped on D4Chess?

Engine-speak on D4Chess is correct and unreadable. Club players would not finish it, let alone trust it. The obvious patch — make it punchy — almost shipped a joke about a blunder that was not in the game.

A coach that lies in plain English is worse than jargon. The player believes it.

What did we change?

A short, human explanation of the game they actually played. They can still open the specialist view if they want. They do not get a second, dishonest personality.

That layer has a name inside the product. The name is not the point. The point is adoption without a second fiction. See what we built.

Personality is optional. Honesty is not.

Why does this show up in custom software?

Portals, notes, and coaches all eventually need a sentence a non-expert will act on. Haven Command already shipped an honest one on a live trainer. That is how we think about AI-powered software — not a walkthrough of how the explanation is assembled.

Questions this note answers

What happens when the explanation is punchy and false?

Jargon nobody finishes, or a personality that invents the facts. Non-experts cannot use either.

What almost shipped on D4Chess?

A punchy explanation of a blunder that never happened. Funny and false.

What does the player experience now?

A human explanation of the real game — or silence. Not a second lie.

How does this show up in custom software?

Every production AI product eventually needs the sentence a non-expert will act on. If that sentence is false, you shipped liability with a tone.
