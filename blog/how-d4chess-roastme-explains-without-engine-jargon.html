Why must a non-expert explanation still be grounded?

Users who are not specialists will not trust +1.4 at move 23. Typical AI hires add personality and invent the facts. RoastMe translates a verified Stockfish line — or it stays quiet.

Shane Foster  ·  Founder, Haven Command LLC  ·  August 17, 2026

What do typical AI hires fail at here?

Fail

They dump the system of record on the user — evals, tokens, JSON — and call it transparency. Or they add a “friendly” voice that invents a blunder so the story lands. Non-experts do the same thing either way: they ignore it, or they paste it into Slack and hope someone else checks.

Explainability is not a meme overlay. It is the sentence a therapist, an operator, or a club player will act on. If that sentence is false, you did not ship trust. You shipped liability with a tone.

What broke in production?

Broke

Engine output on D4Chess is correct and unreadable. A principal variation and an eval tell a club player almost nothing they will finish, let alone trust. The obvious patch — “just roast the game” — reopened the hallucination hole: a punchline about a queen blunder that was not in the game.

A coach that lies in plain English is worse than jargon. The player believes it. Haven Command would not ship a second, ungrounded model just because it shared better.

What did Haven Command change?

Change

RoastMe is a second voice on the same verified Stockfish line. The engine still computes. The coach still stays inside the PV. RoastMe may be short and human — “you hung the exchange on a hope-chess check” — only if that sentence maps to a real moment in the game.

Jargon gets translated, not deleted. The player can still open the line. The roast is the doorway. Distribution without a second, ungrounded model.

Personality is a layer. Honesty is still an engineering problem.
