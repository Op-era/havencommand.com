Why keep undo history after a writer approves an AI rewrite?

Approve is a merge, not a cremation. We keep rewrite history until an explicit save so one green click cannot nuke the draft.

Shane Foster  ·  AI Integrations Engineer, Haven Command LLC  ·  July 27, 2026

What problem did we actually hit?

Problem

The Approve review on ScriptForge AI stopped silent overwrites. It created a new one: Approve felt final. A writer would green-light a rewrite, walk to the kitchen, come back, and realize scene two now belonged to a different movie.

If undo died at Approve, the lock did half its job. We had a human in the loop for one second, then no way back. That is not how people write. It is how people learn to fear the button.

How did Haven Command solve it?

Solution

Approved rewrites stay in history until the writer saves. Approve merges the proposal into the working draft. Save is the commit. Until then, undo can walk the rewrite back like any other edit.

The model is still a petitioner. The difference is time: the writer gets a cooling-off period inside the tool, not a lecture about git. Combined with the always-writable Script tab, the product stays a draft you inhabit.

Approve means merge. Save means commit. Do not collapse those into one irreversible click.

Why does this help someone hiring custom AI work?

For clients

Reversible AI is an ops requirement. Legal, clinical, and editorial tools cannot treat “the model already wrote it” as a commit. I build accept/reject plus history for that reason — on my products first, then on client stacks.

Questions this write-up answers

Why isn’t Approve enough?

Writers approve a pass, then see the damage three beats later. If history died at Approve, the only recovery is memory.

When does ScriptForge drop the undo stack?

At an explicit save — the writer’s decision that this version is the one they are keeping.

What should a client document AI do?

Keep reversible AI edits until a real commit. Treat model output as a branch, not a overwrite of last week’s work.
