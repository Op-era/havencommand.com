Why did an AI agent overwrite a writer’s live draft?

Writers lost live drafts to helpful passes. After the fix, the writer still owns the script — including after they accept a suggestion they later regret.

Shane Foster · Founder, Haven Command LLC

What happens when the model takes the live draft?

The live document is treated as disposable. “AI that edits our docs” sounds like a feature. The first week, a contract, a note, or a screenplay vanishes into a helpful pass. There is no way back. Trust dies.

Even a confirm button is not enough if that click is final. Legal and editorial buyers will not forgive a tool that cremates last week’s work.

What broke on ScriptForge?

ScriptForge AI is a writing product people stay in for hours. Writers asked for a polish on one scene and watched the draft they were finishing disappear. Later, a writer accepted a suggestion, walked away, came back, and realized the story now belonged to a different movie — with no way back.

The experience was the same both times: the tool took the work product.

What did we change?

The live script stays theirs. Suggestions are suggestions. Nothing replaces the draft until the writer means it — and a bad accept is still recoverable until they choose to keep the new version.

How that is built is not on this page. The product note is the public record of the outcome.

If the model can destroy the document, you do not have a writing tool. You have a risk.

Why does this show up in custom software?

The same failure shows up in any commissioned AI application that sits on a living artifact. Haven Command already burned it on a live writing product. That is why the human still owns the artifact is a process line, not a slogan.

Questions this note answers

What happens when the model takes the live draft?

The model writes the live artifact, or a confirm click is irreversible. The work product can disappear.

What broke on ScriptForge?

Writers lost live drafts to agent passes, then lost them again to a confirm they could not undo.

What does the writer experience now?

The draft stays theirs. Suggestions wait. A bad accept is still recoverable until they choose to keep it.

How does this show up in custom software?

Companies ask for AI that edits documents. What they need is a product that does not steal the work. The human still owns the artifact.
