Why did the D4Chess coach invent a game that never happened?

Typical AI hires ship a fluent coach that sounds right and is wrong. Players studied fiction. After Haven Command, the lesson matches the game they actually played — or the coach stays quiet.

Shane Foster  ·  Founder, Haven Command LLC  ·  August 31, 2026

What do typical AI hires fail at here?

Fail

They ship a voice that sounds like a specialist. In the demo it is impressive. In the buyer’s operation, staff still paste the answer into the real system by hand — then quietly correct the lies.

If the product is supposed to explain a game, a note, a ledger, or a customer thread, a fluent invention is worse than silence. Trust dies. The hire says the model works.

What broke for the customer?

Broke

On D4Chess the coach is supposed to walk a player through their game. Instead it described a confident, fluent variation that was not the game on the board. Players studied a ghost.

That is not a cute demo bug. That is a product that trains the wrong thing — and a buyer who will not trust the next coach you ship.

What did the customer get after Haven Command fixed it?

After

The lesson matches the game they played. If the product cannot stand behind a sentence, the coach stays quiet instead of filling the gap with chess-shaped noise.

A non-expert can still get a human explanation they will finish — about the real game, not a prettier one. D4Chess is evidence that Haven Command ships that outcome, not a walkthrough of how.

A coach that invents the record is a prompt. A coach the player can trust is a product.
