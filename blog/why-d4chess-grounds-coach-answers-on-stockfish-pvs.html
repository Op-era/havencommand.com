Insights · D4Chess · August 31, 2026

Why did the D4Chess coach invent a game that never happened?

Players studied a fluent lesson about a game that never happened. After the fix, the coach matches the board they played — or stays quiet.

Shane Foster · Founder, Haven Command LLC

What happens when an AI coach invents the record?

A voice that sounds like a specialist is impressive in a meeting. In the product, people still paste the answer into the real system by hand — then quietly correct the lies.

If the software is supposed to explain a game, a note, a ledger, or a customer thread, a fluent invention is worse than silence. The person on the other side learned the wrong thing. The model is still said to work.

What broke on D4Chess?

On D4Chess the coach is supposed to walk a player through their game. Instead it described a confident, fluent variation that was not the game on the board. Players studied a ghost.

That is not a cute demo bug. That is a product that trains the wrong thing. The next coach you ship inherits the same distrust.

What did we change?

The lesson matches the game they played. If the product cannot stand behind a sentence, the coach stays quiet instead of filling the gap with chess-shaped noise.

A non-expert can still get a human explanation they will finish — about the real game, not a prettier one. How that is enforced is not on this page. The outcome is. See the product note.

A coach that invents the record is a prompt. A coach the player can trust is a product.

Why does this show up in custom software?

Haven Command builds AI applications that have a named job: coach, draft, summarize, schedule. The week after launch, the job is “be true about the record,” not “sound finished.” D4Chess already ran the broken version of that experience and replaced it. The products are the evidence.
