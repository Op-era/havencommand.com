Why does a restart kill multi-user products — including AI ones?

Shared work did not survive a restart. People came back to different realities. After the fix, everyone is on the same island again.

Shane Foster  ·  Founder, Haven Command LLC  ·  June 15, 2026

What happens when a restart splits the world?

Stake

Shared work that only exists while the demo is warm: the room, the session, the in-flight job. A restart, a deploy, a bad night — and every person has a private reality.

Multi-user AI dies the same way. Two operators, one “shared” tool, and after a restart they do not share a product. They share an argument.

What broke for the customer?

Broke

Emoji Isle is a live shared island. After a restart, people were not in the same world: one still standing on a hut the island had forgotten, another back at spawn, a third interacting with a ghost.

That is a game failure. It is also the feeling of an AI room or a shared workflow that “worked in the demo.”

What did the customer get after Haven Command fixed it?

After

After a restart, everyone is on the same island again. A brief interruption — not three private maps.

Emoji Isle is evidence the company treats reliability as the product. It is not a tutorial on how shared worlds are kept.

If people come back to different realities, you do not have a shared product.

What does this prove?

Live

Rooms, sessions, and shared workflows all have to survive a restart. Emoji Isle already paid that debt on a live shared world — and AI rooms fail the same way.

Play the island, or talk to Haven Command if users come back to different truths.
