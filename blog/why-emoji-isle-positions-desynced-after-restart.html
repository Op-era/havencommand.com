Why does a restart kill multi-user products — including AI ones?

Typical AI hires keep agent sessions, rooms, and shared state in memory. A restart invents three realities. Haven Command persisted authority on Emoji Isle and reconciled clients — the same debt that kills multi-user AI.

Shane Foster  ·  Founder, Haven Command LLC  ·  June 15, 2026

What do typical AI hires fail at here?

Fail

They keep the shared record in memory: the agent session, the multiplayer room, the in-flight workflow. It works until a deploy, a crash, or a host recycle. Then every client has a private reality. Reliability is treated as product debt someone else will pay.

Multi-user AI dies the same way. Two operators, two tools, one “source of truth” that was never persisted. After restart they do not share a product. They share an argument.

What broke in production?

Broke

Emoji Isle is a live multiplayer island. After a server restart, positions and world state disagreed: one player still standing on a hut the server had forgotten, another snapping back to spawn, a third interacting with a ghost.

If the authority lived only in memory, a restart was amnesia. Clients filled the gap with whatever they last believed. That is a game bug. It is also the exact failure of an AI room, a shared agent thread, or a collaborative workflow that “worked in the demo.”

What did Haven Command change?

Change

The server’s world is persisted. Restart loads that world. Clients reconcile on reconnect — they do not outvote the authority with a stale transform. Brief interruption beats a permanent private map.

Same family as owning the coach channel and locking a live script: one source of truth, everyone else syncs to it.

If the server forgets the island, the clients will invent three islands. Persist the authority.

Hire
