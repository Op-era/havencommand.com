What other AI engineers tend to fail at

Haven Command ships the finished loop, not a chatbot on a landing page. Typical AI hires fail in modes the company has already cleared:

  • Demos that do not survive production. AutoSocial looked deployed and painted black. A “connected” calendar never posted the week. Buyers inherit a black screen and a meeting that lied.
  • Answers that are not honest. The D4Chess coach invented a game that never happened. Players studied fiction. After the fix, the lesson matches the real game — or the coach stays quiet.
  • AI that steals the work product. ScriptForge writers lost live drafts to “helpful” passes. After the fix, the writer still owns the script.
  • Login theater. The owner could not get into the product they operate. After the fix, the person on the hook can still open the door.
  • Vague tasks with no proof of completion. “Add AI” is not a job. Haven Command takes one named bottleneck with a shipped loop you can point at.
  • Shared work that dies on restart. Emoji Isle players came back to different islands. After the fix, everyone is in the same world again — the same failure multi-user AI hits.

Same class of outcome if Haven Command builds yours: D4Chess coaches from actual mistakes, TheraScribe turns a session into a note the clinician can use, TrustLoopAI keeps an SMS conversation alive when a vendor has a bad day.
